Getting married in Italy as a foreigner: documents, timing and types of ceremony

by Mary Longo, wedding planner and celebrant in Lecce · Updated 27 October 2026

In short

Foreigners can marry in Italy with a civil ceremony (legally binding), a religious one or a symbolic one (no legal effect). For a civil wedding each partner gives the town hall a nulla osta — a declaration that there is no impediment to the marriage — or the equivalent document from their country, plus a passport and, where required, certificates with an apostille and an official Italian translation. Requirements vary by nationality and town hall: start at least 3–4 months ahead.

Which types of ceremony can you choose?

  • Civil ceremony: officiated by the town's civil registrar (or by a person delegated by the mayor) at the town hall or at a venue recognised by the town hall. It is legally binding and can be registered in your home country.
  • Religious ceremony: a Catholic wedding requires documents from your parish, a marriage preparation course and the approval of the dioceses involved; once registered with the town hall it also has civil effect.
  • Symbolic ceremony: it has no legal effect, so it can take place anywhere and in any language. Many couples combine it with a civil ceremony already held at home. More in the guide symbolic vs civil ceremony.

Which documents do you need for a civil wedding?

  • A nulla osta (declaration that there is no impediment to the marriage) or the equivalent document issued by your country, usually through your embassy or consulate.
  • A valid passport.
  • If the town hall requires them: birth certificate, any divorce decree or death certificate of a previous spouse, with an apostille and an official Italian translation.

Some examples: US citizens usually need an Atto Notorio (sworn before an Italian consulate in the USA, valid about 90 days) and a Dichiarazione Giurata (sworn at the US embassy or a US consulate in Italy); British citizens apply for a Certificate of No Impediment, which is converted into a nulla osta following the UK government's instructions. EU citizens usually follow a simpler procedure. Rules change: always check with your consulate and with the chosen town hall.

What are the steps and the timeline?

  1. 6–12 months before: choose the date, the town and the venue (for a civil ceremony outside the town hall, it must be a venue authorised by the town).
  2. 3–4 months before: request the documents in your country, apostilles and translations.
  3. A few days before the wedding: appointment at the town hall to check the documents and make the declaration of intent to marry, with witnesses and, if needed, an interpreter. If neither of you lives in Italy, banns are usually not required in Italy; the town hall will confirm the exact procedure.
  4. The wedding day: civil ceremony with two adult witnesses and, if you do not speak Italian, an interpreter.
